Collective emergence in human-driven and automated traffic

This research investigates microscopic interactions among human-driven and automated vehicles and their implications for collective traffic dynamics, focusing on lane-changing games, individual incentives, system-wide traffic efficiency, and long-tail lane-changing scenarios.

TYPE
Traffic flow dynamics and vehicle behavior
STATUS
Ongoing research; latest results not yet published
WHEN
Since 2016

Research problem

Local decisions by human-driven and automated vehicles jointly influence collective traffic dynamics. This research examines the relationship between individual incentives and system-wide efficiency in lane-changing games, and how microscopic interactions generate and propagate traffic disturbances.

A lane change can reduce the following vehicle's spacing and induce braking. Subsequent car-following responses may propagate the deceleration disturbance upstream, producing local vehicle clustering and affecting traffic efficiency (fig. 1). This mechanism illustrates why individual gains do not necessarily improve system-wide performance.

Current research

Current work focuses on long-tail lane-changing scenarios: infrequent situations that may have substantial effects on traffic operations. It examines their behavioral categories and mechanisms, together with the implications of data scarcity for vehicle behavior modeling.

Research status

The manuscript is in preparation. The latest findings have not yet been published, so quantitative results are not reported here.
